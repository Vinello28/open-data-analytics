Ageing is a complex and natural biological process in which a series of physiological, morphological and biochemical alterations occur ubiquitously in tissues and organs (1). It is marked by a progressive decline in cellular and tissue functions where the underlying mechanisms, despite extensive studies, remain to be elucidated (2-3). An increase of genetic material instability, protein ANNEX B – Project Proposal Page 3 homeostatic loss and cellular senescence are the main causes of the most common age-related diseases such as neurodegeneration, cancer, chronic inflammation, cardiovascular diseases and musculoskeletal disorders (4). Concerning the musculoskeletal system, during ageing changes occur in bones, joints and muscles, which lead to the development of musculoskeletal related diseases. Between them, osteoarthritis (OA) is the most common degenerative joint disorder characterized by the gradual degeneration of all joint resulting in pain, joint stiffness and impaired movement, significantly reducing the quality of life for over 500 million people worldwide (4). OA is highly prevalent among the ageing population (5), often leads to joint pain, disability and a diminished quality of life (6).
