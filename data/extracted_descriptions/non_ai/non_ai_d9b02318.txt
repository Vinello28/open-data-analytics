Quantum many-body batteries (QMBs) are systems described by the laws of quantum mechanics, which can be used to manage energy in nanoscale devices. They are made of qubits, which can exchange energy with a “bath”. In QMBs, collective effects play a pivotal role. In this proposal, we consider two solid-state platforms: circuit quantum electrodynamics architectures based on superconducting circuits and NV centers in diamond. In the first platform, one deals with transmons embedded in a low-loss cavity. In the second one, qubits are the NV centers in diamond, which can be manipulated e.g. via the application of classical external magnetic fields. NV centers are used for quantum sensing and have been proposed as systems for the realization of quantum memories. QMBs have two major objectives here. The first one is to use nonlinearities in superconducting circuits to realize QMBs displaying a genuine quantum advantage. Currently, indeed, the only QMBs where entanglement plays a key role in the charging dynamics are Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev batteries, which are difficult to realize experimentally. The second grand goal we have is to propose and study a multi-functional device based on NV centers in diamond, operating as a battery, memory, and cooperative sensor.
