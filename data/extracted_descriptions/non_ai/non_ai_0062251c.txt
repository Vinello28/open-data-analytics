ECoN aims at studying and developing protocols of entanglement and nonlocality production,
characterization, quantification and activation in complex quantum networks (QNs), as well as
optimization strategies for their purification, distillation, routing, and distribution. It will investigate long-distance entanglement (LDE) preparation and detection in various instances of complex QNs, including: modular arrays of interlinked symmetry-protected topological chains and ladders, each featuring entangled edge modes; homogeneous and inhomogeneous arrays of quantum optical resonators driven by common entangling nonclassical fields; and linked stacks of surface (boundary) spins in homogeneous and inhomogeneous spin QNs. In parallel, ECoN will investigate the possibility of introducing: robust protocols for the generation of end-to-end entanglement at distance-independent rates in a complex QN; optimized schemes for its distribution across the network as a function of realistic network parameters such as entanglement routing probabilities, quantum memory characteristics, and gate fidelities; and protocols for entanglement purification in realistic conditions of noisy gates, depolarizing channels, and finite memory
storage times.
